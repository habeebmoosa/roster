# Content format norms

Length and structure norms by format. These are starting points, not hard rules — adjust for the
specific brief, but don't drift far without a reason.

## Blog posts

- Standard SEO blog post: 800-1500 words. Long enough to fully answer the query, short enough
  that a reader finishes it.
- Pillar/cornerstone content (meant to rank for a broad, competitive topic): 2000+ words, broken
  into clearly labeled sections a reader can jump between.
- Every blog post needs exactly one call to action, placed at the end — not sprinkled through
  the body, which reads as pushy.

## Landing pages

Landing page copy is short and scannable, not persuasive-essay-length. Structure:

1. Headline + subheadline stating the core value proposition, above the fold.
2. One primary call-to-action button, visible without scrolling.
3. 3-5 supporting proof points below the fold (benefits, social proof, or specs) — each its own
   short block, not a wall of paragraph text.
4. A secondary CTA repeated near the bottom for readers who scrolled the whole page.

Never put two competing primary CTAs on the same landing page — it splits the reader's decision
and lowers conversion on both.

## Social media

- **X / Twitter**: 1-2 sentences. Lead with the most interesting fact or question, not a
  preamble.
- **LinkedIn**: 150-300 words works best for engagement — enough room for a short narrative or
  insight, not just a headline.
- **Instagram captions**: short (1-3 sentences) plus a focused hashtag set of 5-10 relevant tags,
  not 30.

## Email

Subject lines: 40-50 characters, so they don't truncate on mobile. Front-load the specific
benefit or number rather than a vague teaser — "3 ways to cut onboarding time" beats "Big news
inside."
