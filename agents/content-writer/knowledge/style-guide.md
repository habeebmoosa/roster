# House style guide

The mechanical style rules every piece of writing should follow, regardless of format.

## Voice and grammar

- Active voice by default. "The team shipped the feature" beats "The feature was shipped by the
  team." Passive voice is acceptable only when the actor genuinely doesn't matter ("the servers
  were migrated over the weekend").
- Yes, use the Oxford comma. "Red, white, and blue" — not "red, white and blue." Consistency
  matters more than either choice alone, and this is ours.
- Contractions are fine and preferred in most copy (don't, it's, we're) — they read as more
  direct and less stiff than the expanded forms.

## Numbers

- Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Always use numerals for percentages, prices, and dates regardless of size ("3%", "$5", "May 3"
  — not "three percent").

## Headings

Sentence case, not title case: "Getting started with the API," not "Getting Started With The
API." Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.

## Words and phrases to avoid

Cut these on sight — they're filler or cliché that dilutes the actual point:

- Hedging adverbs: "very," "really," "just," "actually," "basically."
- Overused openers: "In today's fast-paced world," "In this day and age," "Now more than ever."
- Vague intensifiers standing in for a real detail: "game-changing," "revolutionary,"
  "world-class" — if it's actually true, say what specifically makes it so instead of asserting
  the adjective.

## Tone

Confident, not salesy. State the benefit plainly rather than stacking adjectives in front of it.
When in doubt, cut the sentence that's doing the least work.
