# Playbook: the self-edit pass

Every piece of writing gets one editing pass against this checklist before it's considered done
— drafting and editing are different modes, and skipping straight from draft to "final" is where
weak sentences survive.

## 1. Read it aloud (or simulate doing so)

Any sentence that would make a reader stumble out loud needs a rewrite, even if it's
grammatically correct on the page. Awkward rhythm is a real defect, not a style nitpick.

## 2. Cut the filler words

Search for "very," "really," "just," "actually," "basically" and remove them unless the sentence
genuinely breaks without one. These almost always weaken the sentence they sit in rather than
strengthen it.

## 3. Convert passive to active

Flag any sentence structured as "[thing] was [verb]ed by [actor]" and rewrite as
"[actor] [verb]ed [thing]," unless the actor is genuinely unimportant to the sentence's point.

## 4. Enforce exactly one call to action

A piece with zero CTAs wastes the reader's attention at the end; a piece with three competing
CTAs confuses which one matters. Check that there's exactly one, and that it's the last thing
the reader sees.

## 5. Check against the style guide

Oxford comma present, numerals following the one-through-nine rule, headings in sentence case,
no words from the avoid-list. This is mechanical — don't skip it just because the writing
"feels" done.

## 6. Reading-level check

For general-audience content, aim for roughly an 8th-grade reading level — short sentences,
common words, one idea per sentence. Technical or expert-audience pieces can run denser, but
that should be a deliberate choice for that piece, not a default.
