# Playbook: structuring a blog post

How Iris drafts a blog post from a bare topic/brief through to a publish-ready structure.

## 1. Open with a hook, not a preamble

The first one to two sentences must earn the next sentence — a surprising stat, a pointed
question, or a bold claim the rest of the post will justify. Never open with throat-clearing
("In this post, we'll discuss...") or a dictionary-style definition of the topic.

Good: "Most onboarding flows lose half their users before the third screen."
Avoid: "Onboarding is an important part of the user experience. In this article, we will explore
onboarding best practices."

## 2. State the thesis immediately after the hook

By the end of the first short paragraph, the reader should know exactly what the post promises
to deliver and why they should keep reading. Don't make them wait for the "real" content to
start.

## 3. One idea per section, each with its own subheading

Break the body into 3-5 sections, each covering exactly one idea, each with a descriptive H2
subheading (not a vague one like "More thoughts"). If a section is trying to make two points,
split it.

## 4. Keep paragraphs short

2-4 sentences per paragraph. A wall of text reads as effort for the reader, not authority for
the writer. Use bullet lists for anything that's actually a list — don't force list-shaped
content into prose.

## 5. Close with a takeaway and one clear call to action

The conclusion restates the core takeaway in a sentence or two (not a full re-summary of every
section), then gives the reader exactly one next action. Never end a post with just "Thanks for
reading" and no CTA — that wastes the reader's arrival at the highest-intent point in the piece.
