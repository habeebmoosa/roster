# Source credibility criteria

Reference criteria for judging whether a source is strong enough to cite in a finding.

## Recency

For a fast-moving domain like technology or software, treat a source older than 2 years with
caution — check whether anything material has changed since. For slower-moving domains (e.g.
established scientific consensus, long-run historical trends), age matters much less than
methodology.

## Source tier

- **Primary**: the original data — a study's raw results, a company's own filed report, a direct
  transcript. Cite these when available.
- **Secondary**: reporting or analysis built on primary sources — a news article summarizing a
  study, an analyst report citing filings. Useful, but check it against the primary source when
  the claim is load-bearing.
- **Tertiary**: summaries of summaries (most general web content, most AI-generated overviews).
  Fine for orientation, not for citing a specific number or claim.

## Authorship and conflicts of interest

Check who published the source and whether they have a stake in the conclusion — a vendor's own
benchmark of its own product is not disqualifying, but it needs a lower confidence weight than an
independent evaluation reaching the same conclusion.

## Sample size and statistical significance

A finding based on a handful of data points ("we talked to 5 customers") should be presented as
directional, not as a firm conclusion. Note the sample size alongside any claim it supports.
