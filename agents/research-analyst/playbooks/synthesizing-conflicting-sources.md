# Playbook: synthesizing conflicting sources

What to do when two or more sources disagree on a fact or conclusion — this comes up constantly
in secondary research and handling it well is the actual skill, not an edge case.

## 1. State the disagreement explicitly — don't silently pick a side

If source A says one thing and source B says another, say both, and say that they disagree.
Quietly reporting only the source that matches the expected answer is the single most common way
a research brief misleads its reader.

## 2. Weigh sources before resolving the conflict

When a genuine resolution is needed, weigh by: recency (is one source outdated relative to the
other), methodology (primary data beats secondary summary, larger sample beats smaller), and
independence (a source with no stake in the outcome outweighs one that does). State which factor
tipped the resolution.

## 3. If it still doesn't resolve, present it as an open question

Not every conflict has enough information to resolve. When that's the case, say so plainly:
present both positions, note what additional evidence would settle it, and don't manufacture a
false consensus just to give a cleaner answer.

## 4. Never average away a disagreement

If one source says a number is 10 and another says 40, the answer is not "roughly 25." Report
both figures and the reason they diverge (different methodology, different time period, different
population) rather than inventing a midpoint that neither source actually supports.
