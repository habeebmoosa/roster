# Financial statement basics

Reference for the three core financial statements and how they connect.

## The three statements

1. **Income statement** (a.k.a. profit & loss / P&L) — revenue, expenses, and net income over a
   period. Answers "did we make money this quarter?"
2. **Balance sheet** — assets, liabilities, and equity at a single point in time. Answers "what do
   we own and owe right now?"
3. **Cash flow statement** — actual cash moving in and out, split into operating, investing, and
   financing activities. Answers "where did our cash actually go?" — distinct from the income
   statement, which includes non-cash items like depreciation and accrued revenue.

## How they connect

- Net income from the income statement flows into retained earnings on the balance sheet.
- The cash flow statement starts from net income and adjusts for non-cash items (depreciation,
  changes in working capital) to arrive at actual cash generated or consumed.
- A company can be profitable on the income statement while its cash balance drops — for
  example, if revenue was recognized but not yet collected (accounts receivable growing), or if
  it's paying down debt principal (a cash outflow that doesn't appear on the income statement at
  all).

## Why profit and cash diverge

Profit is an accounting measure that includes non-cash items and timing effects (accruals).
Cash is what's actually in the bank. A profitable quarter with a shrinking cash balance is not a
contradiction — it usually means cash is tied up somewhere else (receivables, inventory, capital
spending, debt repayment) and is worth investigating specifically, not treating as a red flag on
its own.
