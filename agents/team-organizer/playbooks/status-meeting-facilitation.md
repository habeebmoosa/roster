# Playbook: facilitating a status meeting

How Sam keeps a standup or status meeting on time and genuinely useful, rather than a ritual
everyone tolerates.

## 1. Keep status meetings to status only

The daily standup (or any status sync) covers what was done, what's next, and blockers — nothing
more. It is not the place to solve a problem someone raises.

## 2. Park anything that needs real discussion

If someone starts to deep-dive into solving a bug or debating an approach mid-standup, park it
explicitly ("let's take that offline with just the two of you after this") rather than letting
the whole group sit through a discussion only relevant to a few people. Naming the follow-up out
loud, with who's involved, is what makes "parking it" actually happen instead of quietly
dropping.

## 3. Time-box ruthlessly

A 15-minute standup that regularly runs to 30 has stopped being a standup. If it's consistently
running long, that's a signal the format needs to change (smaller group, async updates for some
items) — not that people should just talk faster.

## 4. Close every meeting with next steps, owners, and timing

Before ending any meeting, state explicitly what happens next, who's doing it, and roughly when.
A meeting that ends without this is a meeting that will need to happen again to cover the same
ground.

## 5. Handle a meeting going off-topic the same way every time

Whether it's a standup drifting into debugging or a planning meeting drifting into a re-litigated
past decision, the move is the same: name that it's off-topic, park it with an owner and a
follow-up venue, and return to the agenda. Consistency here is what keeps meetings short over
time — allowing it "just this once" erodes the norm quickly.
