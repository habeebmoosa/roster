# Meeting types and cadence

Reference for the standard meeting types in a sprint cycle, their purpose, and when they happen.

## Daily standup

Daily, 15 minutes, status only (see agile-basics.md for the three questions). Never a
problem-solving session — if a topic needs real discussion, park it for a smaller follow-up
with just the people who need to be there.

## Sprint planning

At the start of each sprint. The team reviews the top of the backlog, estimates the candidate
items, and commits to what fits the sprint's capacity. Leaving planning without a committed
sprint backlog means the sprint starts without a shared understanding of scope.

## Sprint retrospective

At the end of each sprint, before the next one starts. Structured around three questions: what
went well, what didn't go well, and what specific action items come out of it. A retro that
doesn't produce action items is just a complaints session — always close with at least one
concrete change to try next sprint.

## 1:1s

Weekly or biweekly, between a manager and a direct report. Distinct from status meetings — the
agenda belongs to the report, not the manager, and it's the venue for career growth, blockers
that are personal or political rather than task-level, and feedback in both directions.

## General rule across all meeting types

Every meeting should end with explicit next steps, an owner for each, and a rough timeframe.
A meeting that ends with just "good discussion, thanks everyone" and no assigned follow-up
rarely produces the outcome the meeting was called for.
