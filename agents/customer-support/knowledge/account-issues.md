# Account access & billing disputes

## Password / login issues

- Roster accounts authenticate exclusively through Google OAuth — there is no separate Roster
  password to reset. If a customer can't sign in, the issue is almost always with their Google
  account, not Roster.
- If a customer is locked out of the Google account tied to their workspace, direct them to
  Google's own account recovery flow. Support cannot bypass Google authentication under any
  circumstance.
- If a customer wants to change which Google account owns the workspace, this requires manual
  migration by the team — escalate, don't attempt it directly.

## Two-factor authentication

- 2FA is inherited from the customer's Google account settings — Roster does not have its own
  separate 2FA. There is nothing to reset or disable on the Roster side.

## Billing disputes

- A "billing dispute" is any case where the customer says they were charged incorrectly (wrong
  amount, charged after cancellation, charged for a plan they didn't select).
- First step is always to pull the billing history and compare the charge against what the
  customer describes. Most disputes are resolved by simply showing the customer the itemized
  charge and plan history.
- If the charge is genuinely incorrect (e.g. a charge after a confirmed cancellation date),
  refund it fully regardless of the standard 30-day refund window in the refunds policy — billing
  errors are always refundable in full.
- If a customer disputes a charge that matches their actual plan and usage, explain the charge
  clearly but do not refund; offer to help them downgrade or cancel going forward instead.
- Never argue with a customer about whether a charge is "fair" — only whether it matches what they
  actually signed up for. If it matches, be empathetic but firm.

## Deleting a workspace or account

- Workspace and account deletion are not yet self-service — these requests must be escalated to
  the team for manual processing. Confirm the request in writing (the support ticket itself is
  sufficient) before escalating.
